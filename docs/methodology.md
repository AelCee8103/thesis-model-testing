# Model Deployment

After the trained checkpoints were saved to disk, the proposed Tree-Structured Regional CNN-BiLSTM and the baseline TSR-CNN-LSTM were deployed through a lightweight inference dashboard so that both architectures could be queried on demand and their predictions compared side by side on previously unseen text. The deployed system accepts a user-supplied sentence, allows the operator to select the dataset, the predicted dimension, and the architecture, and returns the model's predicted score on the dataset's native rating scale together with the bag-of-leaves regions extracted by the constituency parser at the dataset's optimal tree depth. The same English sentence — *"The team worked overtime for weeks, and when the launch finally succeeded everyone cried with joy."* — was submitted to the deployed system twice, once against the baseline architecture and once against the proposed architecture, with all other settings held identical so that the difference between the two predictions is attributable solely to the LSTM-to-BiLSTM swap.

The first submission used the Stanford Sentiment Treebank (SST) dataset for valence prediction with the baseline LSTM architecture and the *Paper* training configuration, as shown in Figure 1. The constituency parser produced sixteen regions out of the model's trained capacity of twenty-three, encoding the eighteen-token input at the dataset's optimal tree depth of five. The baseline TSR-CNN-LSTM returned the prediction shown in Figure 2: a valence score of **0.54** on SST's `[0, 1]` scale, with a raw model output of **0.5361**, which the dashboard's interpretation routine mapped to the *Neutral* band. Although the input sentence reads as unambiguously positive to a human annotator, the baseline architecture placed it only marginally above the midpoint of the scale, indicating that the unidirectional aggregation step was unable to fully integrate the affective signal carried by the clause-final phrase *"cried with joy"* into the document representation.

The same sentence was then submitted under an identical configuration with the architecture switched to the proposed BiLSTM (Figure 3). The preprocessing pipeline produced the same sixteen regions over the same eighteen tokens at the same target depth, ensuring that the two architectures saw an identical input. The proposed Tree-Structured Regional CNN-BiLSTM returned the prediction shown in Figure 4: a valence score of **0.66** on SST's `[0, 1]` scale, with a raw model output of **0.6635**, which the dashboard mapped to the *Mildly positive* band. The proposed architecture therefore produced a score that was approximately **0.12** higher than the baseline on the same input, and crossed the interpretation threshold separating *Neutral* from *Mildly positive* — a qualitative shift in the rendered label that the operator can read directly from the dashboard's coloured score gauge without inspecting the underlying number.

This deployment-time comparison illustrates, in a setting that closely mirrors how an end user would interact with the system, the mechanism by which bidirectional aggregation modifies the model's behaviour. Because the proposed architecture passes information through the regional sequence in both directions, the affective contribution of the clause-final phrase *"cried with joy"* is incorporated into every region of the resulting document representation, including the early regions corresponding to the relatively neutral phrase *"The team worked overtime for weeks"*. The unidirectional baseline, by contrast, can only integrate that final-clause signal once it has already finished aggregating the earlier regions, and consequently produces a more conservative prediction that sits closer to the dataset midpoint. The 0.12-point gap between the two predictions on this single test case is consistent with the broader pattern observed during the formal experimental evaluation, in which the proposed BiLSTM tended to produce more decisively polarised predictions on inputs whose dominant affective cue lay near the end of the sentence. The fact that this behaviour is also visible in the deployed dashboard — and that the dashboard surfaces it in a form (the *Neutral* → *Mildly positive* label transition) that a non-technical observer can interpret at a glance — provides an additional, qualitative line of evidence for the contribution of the proposed architecture beyond the quantitative metrics reported in the experimental evaluation.
